## What I learned across the full 60 days

Four things distilled from every LinkedIn post I wrote, all 10 capstone days, and this final writeup. These are what I want to remember when the details fade:

**1. Non-developers can ship real products now.** Not with hand-waving. With actual production apps, hardened, deployed, at zero cost. The tooling collapse over the last 18 months is real, and it is the single most important skill leverage a non-CS person can pick up right now.

**2. Deploys lie. Curl doesn't.** Every deploy log says "success" when the platform's build completed, not when the product works. The Day 59 404 catch is the single most valuable lesson in this entire capstone. Bake verification into every ship.

**3. Constraint is a design tool.** "Free tier only." "No signup." "No framework." Every constraint made Kickoff sharper and easier to distribute. Write the constraints before the features on every future project.

**4. The habit compounds.** 60 consecutive days of shipping something small in public trained a muscle I didn't have on Day 1. That muscle — showing up on the tired days, telling the truth in the failure posts, closing the laptop on time — is the actual asset from this challenge. Everything else is downstream.
